APAC Alpha-to-Portfolio

Do Momentum and Low-Risk signals generalize across APAC equities, and can they be translated into an economically useful OOS portfolio?

  • 7 markets
  • 210 stocks
  • 2010–2026 research
  • 2016–2026 OOS / 2026 partial
  • Momentum + Low Risk
  • Survivor-biased exploratory sample

A small, pre-specified set of equity return predictors is tested on a monthly panel of CN, HK, IN, JP, KR, SG and TW, then translated into long-only country-sleeve portfolios and evaluated out-of-sample in USD, net of illustrative frictions and dated taxes. Momentum is the strongest signal and the only member of the primary family to survive the project’s own multiplicity correction; Low Risk lowers realised volatility but its return premium is inverted in this panel; and the long-only optimizer does not beat equal-weight 1/N out-of-sample. No result here is a bias-free historical APAC universe, and the sample carries survivorship bias.

Three conclusions

Momentum is the strongest signal and survives the project's own correction.

Pooled country-equal rank IC 0.0331; Fama–MacBeth slope 0.0031 (NW HAC(6) t = 3.30) — the only member of the four-test primary family that survives the Sidak/Bonferroni correction. Q5−Q1 is positive in 6 of 7 markets (India marginally negative). Net OOS Momentum CAGR 22.6%, Sharpe 1.07.

Low Risk lowers volatility but its premium is inverted in this panel.

LOWRISK IC 0.0022; Fama–MacBeth slope −0.0026 (NW HAC(6) t = −2.31). Q5−Q1 is negative in 7 of 7 markets — high-volatility names out-returned low-vol names. The sleeve's value is risk reduction, not alpha (12.9% volatility, the lowest of the six).

The optimizer fails to beat 1/N out-of-sample.

Optimized net CAGR 12.5% vs 1/N 15.9%; Sharpe 0.76 vs 0.89; net drawdown worse. A claim of improvement is made only if OOS net economics improve after the corrected per-one-way turnover charge — here they do not. Lower volatility (13.9% vs 15.5%) is the sole improvement.

Cleaning & Investability

ri,t = Padji,t / Padji,t−1 − 1
  • ri,t monthly total return of name i in calendar month t.
  • Padji,t split- and dividend-adjusted close at the month-end of t.
  • Padji,t−1 adjusted close at the immediately preceding calendar month-end.
  • Valid only when t−1 is the immediately preceding calendar month; no forward fill, no interpolation.
What
Daily → monthly total returns from Adj Close on actual month-end observations. No forward fill, no interpolation. TRADED / STALE / SUSPENDED states; corporate actions already embedded in Adj Close and never re-applied; fixed lagged bottom-20% liquidity filter; survivorship flagged, never cured.
Formula
monthly_total_return = adj_close_t / adj_close_{t-1} − 1 only where the prior observation is in the immediately preceding calendar month.
Why
Avoid stale/unadjusted vendor prices and survivor-only universes; keep a point-in-time investability gate.
Literature
Shumway (1997) delisting bias; Dimson (1979) infrequent trading; Hou, Xue & Zhang (2020) investability; Ince & Porter (2006).

Signal Construction

MOMi,t = ∏m=t−12t−2(1 + ri,m) − 1
LOWRISKi,t = −√252 · sd(ri,d) over 60 traded sessions (min 40)
Compositei,t = 0.5·z(MOM) + 0.5·z(LOWRISK)
  • i security; t formation month.
  • m monthly index over the 11-cell window t−12..t−2 (t−1 skipped); ri,m monthly total return.
  • d daily index over the trailing 60 traded sessions (minimum 40); ri,d daily total return.
  • ∏ product across the window; sd sample standard deviation.
  • z cross-sectional standardisation within market-month.
What
MOM = 12-1 momentum (skip the latest month). LOWRISK = −vol_60; secondary vol_252, beta, IVOL. Composite = fixed 0.5·z(MOM)+0.5·z(LOWRISK). All trailing and point-in-time.
Formula
mom_12_1 = prod_{m=t-12}^{t-2}(1+r_m) − 1 (11 cells, month t-1 excluded); vol_60 = sqrt(252)·std(daily traded returns), min 40 traded sessions.
Why
Small, pre-specified, transparent signals — no zoo mining, no composite re-weighting.
Literature
Jegadeesh & Titman (1993); Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013); Ang, Hodrick, Xing & Zhang (2006); Frazzini & Pedersen (2014).

IC / Quintiles

ICc,t = Spearman(Si,t, Ri,t+1)
Spreadc,t+1 = RQ5 − RQ1
  • c market; t month; i security.
  • Si,t signal value; Ri,t+1 next-month return.
  • Spearman rank correlation computed within market-month.
  • Q5, Q1 highest- and lowest-expected-return quintile buckets; Spread = Q5−Q1.
What
Per market-month rank IC and Q1..Q5 buckets; Q5−Q1 spread; country-equal pooled aggregate only after country results.
Formula
IC = Spearman(signal, next_month_return) within market-month; Q5 = highest-expected-return bucket.
Why
Rank measures are robust to outliers and non-normality; separate long-short spread from implementable long-only sleeve.
Literature
Fama & French (2012) on international cross-sections.

Fama–MacBeth (primary)

Ri,t+1 = at + bM,t MOM_zi,t + bL,t LOWRISK_zi,t + γt′ Controlsi,t + δc,t + εi,t+1
  • Ri,t+1 next-month return; at monthly intercept.
  • bM,t, bL,t monthly slopes on MOM and LOWRISK.
  • MOM_zi,t, LOWRISK_zi,t within-market standardised signals.
  • γt control-slope vector; Controls = lagged-return z and log-liquidity z.
  • δc,t market fixed effect; εi,t+1 residual.
  • Monthly slopes are averaged; standard errors use Newey–West HAC(6).
What
Monthly cross-sectional OLS of next-month return on within-market standardised mom_z, lowrisk_z and two controls; market fixed effects; mean of monthly slopes with Newey–West HAC(6) standard errors.
Formula
r_{i,t+1} = a + β_MOM z(MOM)_{i,t} + β_LR z(LOWRISK)_{i,t} + γ′controls + marketFE + e_{i,t+1}.
Why
Two-pass standard; within-market standardisation limits currency/market artefacts; NW HAC(6) corrects autocorrelation.
Literature
Fama & MacBeth (1973); Newey & West (1987).

OOS Forecast

μ̂i,t = b̂≤t−1′ zi,t
  • μ̂i,t forecast next-month return for name i at formation month t.
  • b̂≤t−1 slope estimate trained only on formation months up to and including t−1.
  • zi,t contemporaneous within-market standardised signal vector.
  • Expanding window uses no future values; an inadequate history marks the sleeve-month NA rather than trading.
What
Expanding-window Fama–MacBeth coefficients trained only on past formation months whose next-month return is already realised; forecast = slopes · z_i(t). If history is inadequate the sleeve-month is not traded (marked NA, no silent fallback).
Formula
μ_i(t) = slopes(t) · z_i(t).
Why
Strictly past data only; expanding window — no re-selection.
Literature
Fama & MacBeth (1973).

Portfolio Construction

maxw w′μ̂ − λ w′Σ̂ w − η c · 0.5 Σi | wi − w_previ | subject to Σi wi = 1, wi ≥ 0
  • w long-only weight vector; μ̂ expected returns.
  • Σ̂ Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage covariance of trailing USD returns.
  • λ = 5 risk-aversion coefficient; η = 1 turnover-penalty weight.
  • c one-way cost (15 bps base); w_prev prior-month weights.
  • Fully invested, non-negative weights solved with SLSQP.
What
Country sleeves built monthly from eligible names, combined equal-country: 1/N, Momentum top quintile, Low Risk top quintile, Composite top quintile, and a long-only mean–variance Optimized sleeve.
Formula
max w·μ − λ w′Σw − η c · 0.5 Σ|w − w_prev| s.t. Σw=1, w≥0, λ=5, Ledoit–Wolf Σ, SLSQP.
Why
Translate statistical signals into investable, fully-invested, long-only portfolios.
Literature
Markowitz (1952); Ledoit & Wolf (2004); DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009); Kraft (1988).

Data & cleaning

Market/sample coverage, source hierarchy and the investability gate. QC images are selected from reports/charts/cleaning/.

Coverage & source hierarchy

  • Markets: CN, HK, IN, JP, KR, SG, TW. 210 research-panel securities; 222 master rows, including 214 common equities and 8 excluded investment vehicles.
  • Primary source: yfinance for every market. BaoStock / AKShare are CN/HK cross-checks only and never overwrite the primary series.
  • No forward fill, no interpolation. Month-end = last actual trading observation with an immediately preceding calendar-month pair.
  • Trading state distinguishes TRADED / STALE / SUSPENDED from provider flags and volume — never inferred from returns.
  • Corporate actions: total returns use vendor Adj Close; dividends/splits are not re-applied (corporate_events_applied_again = False).
  • Investability: fixed lagged bottom-20% liquidity filter within market-month — a control, never optimised.
  • Survivorship: flagged in all seven markets, not cured; CN/HK delisting coverage is 0/N.

References (recorded DOIs): Ince & Porter 2006, Shumway 1997, Dimson 1979, Hou, Xue & Zhang 2020.

Signals

Fixed, transparent, orientation-uniform: higher = higher expected return / lower risk.

MOM — 12-1 momentum

mom_12_1 = ∏_{m=t-12}^{t-2}(1+r_m) − 1

Compounds exactly the 11 monthly total returns t-12..t-2; the most recent month t-1 is skipped. Built on a gap-aware grid — all 11 cells must be present.

LOWRISK — low volatility

LOWRISK = −vol_60

Annualised standard deviation of daily total returns over 60 traded sessions (sqrt(252), min 40). Secondary: vol_252, beta, and idiosyncratic volatility (CN beta/IVOL are NA — the CN benchmark has one row).

Composite — fixed 50/50

0.5·z(MOM) + 0.5·z(LOWRISK)

A transparent combined score, fixed ex ante and not re-optimised on history. Z-scores are cross-sectional within market-month.

Signal testing

Rank IC, quintile spreads and pooled Fama–MacBeth. Evidence is gross / pre-cost at this stage.

External validation (JKP)

Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen country momentum and low_risk factors on exact common month-ends.

Validation, not replication. JKP factors are value-weighted long–short country factors in decimal excess USD. This project's series are equal-weight, survivor-biased Q5−Q1 spreads on a 210-name current universe. Methodology, weighting and units differ, so agreement is corroborating rather than a replication. French is used only for a market-return cross-check (it carries no momentum/low-risk); the ex-Japan aggregate is not HK/SG. AQR is NOT_AVAILABLE (not in approved inputs; no network substitute).

Out-of-sample construction

Strictly past-information flow. OOS is 2016-01..2026-09, 129 months; 2026 is a partial year and labelled incomplete throughout.

  1. Past data onlyFormation month t sees only realised history.
  2. Expanding FM forecastsμ_i(t) = slopes(t)·z_i(t); NA rather than a silent fallback.
  3. Ledoit–Wolf covarianceTrailing 36 monthly USD returns, min 24 obs, common-asset sample.
  4. PortfolioLong-only, fully invested; turnover penalty uses the same per-one-way convention as realised costs.
  5. Future periodWeights formed at end of t earn realised month t+1.

Objective: max w·μ − λ w′Σw − ηcΣ|w−w_prev|, λ=5, η=1, c=15 bps, subject to Σw=1, w≥0. The benchmark is 6-market (HK, IN, JP, KR, SG, TW) because the CN benchmark has only one row and no synthetic China is created.

Literature: Markowitz (1952); Ledoit & Wolf (2004); DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal (2009); trading-cost references as recorded in project docs.

Limitations

Survivor-biased exploratory evidence, not a bias-free historical APAC universe.

  • Current-universe survivorship in all seven markets. CN and HK carry a questionable label with 0/N delisting coverage; no market is survivorship-free.
  • Only 210 curated names — not exhaustive historical listings; OOS claims are conditional on this sample.
  • Incomplete delistings and inert listing/delisting gates (statuses carry no parseable dates).
  • Single price source for most markets; independent cross-check exists only for CN and HK.
  • CN beta / IVOL unavailable — the CN benchmark has a single daily row; CN is excluded from market-model signals only.
  • Incomplete historical taxes — only CN/HK have verified effective dates; all other jurisdictions are not applied.
  • 2026 is partial (9 months) and flagged incomplete throughout.
